Manuscript Overview & Core Contributions:
High-stakes decision support systems (such as credit scoring, fraud detection, healthcare, and industrial safety) demand distribution-free uncertainty quantification under severe class imbalance and asymmetric error costs. While standard marginal conformal prediction guarantees valid overall coverage, it systematically under-covers rare, costly positive classes (averaging only 30.77% minority coverage across 3,150 experimental fits in our multi-domain benchmark).

In this work, we present a systematic multi-domain benchmark across 15 imbalanced tabular datasets, 7 classifiers, 3 probability calibrators, and 10 random seeds. Our main contributions include:
1. Multi-Domain Benchmark & Coverage Restoration: We demonstrate that class-conditional (Mondrian) conformal prediction restores minority-class validity, achieving 92.12% mean minority coverage (+61.35 percentage-point gain over marginal CP).
2. Selection-Split Calibration Protocol: We formalize a split-conformal protocol that decouples miscoverage selection from final quantile estimation while maintaining class-conditional validity guarantees.
3. Capacity-Constrained Cost Modeling & Operational Boundaries: We model cost-sensitive human deferral under review overheads (C_rev) and human queue capacity limits (r_max = 10%), evaluate reviewer error rates, and derive closed-form empirical break-even thresholds (C_rev^*). This establishes explicit boundary conditions where human expert deferral is economically viable versus automated Bayes point classification.
